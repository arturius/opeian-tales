Cities:

The island of Tenedad has some truly unique assets that have garnered persistent attention over the ages. Once referred to as the “Feywild of Opeia,” the region exhibits a handful of physiographical anomalies presenting in strange and enticing landscapes with magic permeating the air.

Prior to the Peltran invasion during the 9th century, Tenedad was a proud, whimsical nation of wonderment—skeptical mages, broken soldiers, and passionate artists traveled there to experience something peculiar and pay their respects to a land woven between Ovphy and Zeune’s twin forces.

The annexation of the island spread great turmoil among Tenedadians, resulting in frequent civilian uprisings and the assembly of various resistance groups. The Peltran presence incited such hatred that The Legion’s takeover a mere century later was partially aided by the rebellion, viewed as a favored outcome to the Kingdom’s rule.

![Tenedad](https://opeiantales.blog/wp-content/themes/magnumopus/assets/png/regtenedad.png)

Alas, Tenedadian hopes had proven to be nothing but the desperate strung along by false promises of liberation, as The Legion had no such plans in mind. Since the faction’s seizing of the reins, Tenedad has gradually regressed into a state of anarchy devoid of regulations.

With The Legion’s leaders hidden away in the sealed capital, the rest of the island receives next to no care or support. Roaming packs of bandits plague the roads and mobsters vie for control in every city as theft, extortion, and murder become basic means of survival.

Tenedad’s most prized resource is a mineral aptly named Tenedite, famed for its versatile properties. While it can be found in abundance across the island, The Legion has claimed monopoly over this valuable material.

The crystal can be purchased from approved traders throughout the continent and is a popular baseline among weaponsmiths and jewelers alike. Due to its strong cultural significance, many natives possess some form of Tenedite adornment.

Located northwest of the mainland, Tenedad experiences hot summers and cold winters, with sharp temperature shifts between day and night. The terrain consists of dunes, rolling plains, and mountainous regions.

Rainfall varies significantly, with the northwest receiving the most precipitation. The Carmis Valley, famed for its wine, is among the driest regions on the island.
